# Example A: AI short drama

**Route:** existing-industry structural change  
**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-09-29  
**Boundary:** China mobile micro-drama and AI comic-drama workflows using advertising, in-app payment, licensing, or platform revenue share  
**Purpose:** demonstrate bottleneck migration after production cost falls; not investment advice

## Review card

**Memory sentence:** AI makes ordinary footage cheaper to produce, but it does not make attention, platform acceptance, monetization, or reusable audience feedback abundant.

```text
Y = a repeatable portfolio of AI-assisted short dramas that passes rights and
    content review and reaches platform retention or monetization thresholds

Q = validated narrative appeal and monetizable attention

Omega = China mobile distribution, IAA/IAP/revenue-share models,
        observed through 2026-09-29
```

### Baseline and change

| Layer | 2024-2025 baseline | After scalable AI generation | Structural effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical production | Fast but still coordinates actors, location, camera, costume, editing, and sunk shooting cost | More scenes and variants can be produced by a small team | Widened for ordinary content |
| Visual delivery | Mature regional production capacity | Model control, character continuity, and rework become temporary local constraints | Constraint changes composition |
| Story and aesthetic acceptance | Script hooks and editing rhythm already matter | Cheap supply raises the value of deciding what is worth generating | Relative importance rises |
| Platform acceptance | Review, rights checks, recommendation, and paid traffic determine launch and reach | AI labeling and provenance add requirements; supply grows faster | Remains a hard gate |
| Commercial validation | Exposure, retention, payment, and ad yield determine whether to continue | More low-cost variants compete for limited user time | Becomes relatively narrower |
| Feedback ownership | Platforms and integrated content operators see user behavior | Faster iteration is valuable only when feedback can modify the next version | Stronger leverage and capture |

### Canonical chain

```text
story/IP
-> asset and shot definition
-> model-assisted generation
-> human continuity and aesthetic acceptance
-> rights/content review
-> platform recommendation and acquisition test
-> advertising/payment/licensing result
-> retention and conversion feedback
-> next story, cut, asset, and campaign decision
```

The production change closes a short local loop: generate, review, modify. Industry profit changes only after the output crosses the platform bridge and returns audience and revenue feedback.

## Bottleneck judgment

| Node | Current interpretation | Capture boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation video model/API | Important input; improving substitution and falling unit cost weaken a permanent single-vendor cut | Model revenue does not prove control of short-drama profit |
| Controllable workflow and human acceptance | Current local constraint for continuity and rework | Know-how may productize and diffuse |
| Ordinary production capacity | Increasingly replaceable | Weak capture without audience, rights, or a repeatable hit record |
| Clear and validated IP/story pattern | More valuable than undifferentiated text inventory | Capture depends on rights and demonstrated conversion |
| Content, rights, and AI-label review | High gate strength | Regulators do not privately capture the rent; platforms gain operating control by bearing responsibility |
| Recommendation, monetization, and user data | Strongest current commercial waist | Platforms combine entry, ranking, pricing, feedback, and removal rights |

**Current class:** production relaxation plus bottleneck migration, with a platform-centered feedback cone and small emergent clusters of proven IP, workflow, and distribution.

## What is fact, inference, and unknown

- `[F]` Public industry and company sources document mature short-drama distribution, platform review responsibilities, expanding free/ad-supported viewing, growing AI supply, and examples of lower production cost.
- `[I]` When eligible content supply grows faster than attention and monetization, recommendation and feedback become relatively more constraining.
- `[U]` Comparable industry-wide medians for AI project profitability, per-title exposure, rework cost, and platform bargaining outcomes remain unavailable.

## Strongest objection

AI may eventually improve story selection and generation together, while multi-platform distribution or direct subscriptions weaken the present platform waist. Lower production cost could also expand total viewing and advertising enough that the apparent attention constraint loosens.

## Falsifiers and update triggers

Revise the conclusion when one or more of these become observable:

1. independent producers retain portable audience data and similar monetization across platforms;
2. median qualified-title exposure and profitability rise with supply rather than fall or concentrate;
3. one-click workflows remove meaningful differences in continuity, editing, and rework;
4. original low-cost tests persistently outperform licensed IP, reducing the premium on proven stories;
5. recommendation and platform revenue share cease to dominate commercial acceptance.

## Why this example belongs

It prevents a simple “cost down equals producer profit up” story. The changed node is real, the product loop is externally visible, and the successor constraints can be checked with platform, producer, and audience evidence.
